How this experiment works

Combined mode tests candidate rules, not final Curate behavior. It joins supported pairs before attaching uncertain photos and checks every cross-group separation. Different Enrich categories corroborated by recognition counts can split a shared-backdrop group. Returned rank contrast with supported alternatives can corroborate a people or visual difference; not-returned results stay unknown. Reciprocal near ranks conflicting with people evidence stay uncertain.

ThumbHash has three bands: very close can support a pair without other signals, the middle band needs corroboration, and clearly different can corroborate separation. Other signals can still conflict with those observations. Ranks exclude other photos in this selected time group when counting outside photos ahead. This compensates for burst size, but does not prove the excluded photos are true alternatives or turn ranks into distances. All cutoffs need evaluation.

Ranks are held fixed after each explicit pass. Completed rows survive cancellation or failure; remaining references stay unqueried. Failed references are not retried by “Check remaining”; reset evidence to deliberately revisit them. Reset may reuse a valid 10-minute cache. Closing this window stops further searches. The two AI referees are not called.

In individual-filter mode, photos are processed oldest first and join the most recently created compatible group. The gap is measured from that group’s last photo; the span from its first. ThumbHash, people-category and recognized-identity checks apply to every member. Changing a threshold can rearrange the partitions rather than just remove photos.

This is an experimental grouping rule, not an exact replay of either Curate version. ThumbHash is a rough visual descriptor, not an exact duplicate checksum or confidence score. In individual-filter mode, missing or incompatible hashes stay separate when the hash rule is on. In combined mode they stay unknown. Saved stack corrections and duplicate IDs are ignored here. These rules only split the selected time group; widen the starting gap on the main page to explore other neighbors.

Person 1, Person 2, etc. refer to the same Immich identities throughout this experiment, not across different experiments. Recognition can be incomplete or wrong: even disjoint lists can describe different detected people in the same scene. Matching identities do not prove similar composition.

Opening a group refreshes its recognized identities from Immich. Other evidence comes from the time-group snapshot. The experiment stays fixed until you explicitly refresh recognition or rebuild time groups. A refresh may wait briefly to avoid repeating requests too quickly. Failed or missing recognition stays unknown; it never falls back to old identities. No photos are deleted or marked reviewed.
