How this experiment works

Photos are processed oldest first and join the most recently created compatible group. The gap is measured from that group’s last photo; the span from its first. ThumbHash, people-category and recognized-identity checks apply to every member. Changing a threshold can rearrange the partitions rather than just remove photos.

This is an experimental grouping rule, not an exact replay of either Curate version. ThumbHash is a rough visual descriptor, not an exact duplicate checksum or confidence score. Missing or incompatible hashes stay separate when the hash rule is on. Saved stack corrections and duplicate IDs are ignored here. These rules only split the selected time group; widen the starting gap on the main page to explore other neighbors.

Person 1, Person 2, etc. refer to the same Immich identities throughout this experiment, not across different experiments. Recognition can be incomplete or wrong: even disjoint lists can describe different detected people in the same scene. Matching identities do not prove similar composition.

Opening a group refreshes its recognized identities from Immich. Other evidence comes from the time-group snapshot. The experiment stays fixed until you explicitly refresh recognition or rebuild time groups. A refresh may wait briefly to avoid repeating requests too quickly. Failed or missing recognition stays unknown; it never falls back to old identities. No photos are deleted or marked reviewed.
